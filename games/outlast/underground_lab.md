## 1. Wernicke's project

### Description

Miles learns about Project Walrider.

### Observation

Miles learns that the Walrider was created by Dr. Rudolf Wernicke, a German scientist brought to the United States through Operation Paperclip. Wernicke believed that intensive dream therapy on traumatized patients could connect swarms of nanites into a single being. Miles also learns that the experiments were originally part of the MKUltra program. The Walrider is linked to the patient Billy Hope, who lies in a glass pod on life support.

### Interpretation

Trauma is not a side effect of the experiment but its precondition: the patients are traumatized because traumatized patients are the material the technology needs. See [torture as research](../../tags/torture.md#torture-as-research). The references to Operation Paperclip and MKUltra place the fiction in the real history of US research on human subjects; see also [Documents](documents.md), section 2.

## 2. Billy Hope's life support

### Description

Wernicke gives Miles a task.

### Observation

Wernicke tells Miles to find Billy Hope in the laboratory and shut off his life support, so that the Walrider dies with him. The player shuts off the life support. Before Hope dies, the Walrider attacks Miles and possesses him. On the way out, Wernicke and armed guards shoot Miles. Wernicke's voice recognizes that Miles is the Walrider's new host; gunfire, screams and mauling sounds follow, then the credits.

### Interpretation

At the end, the player kills the last test subject on the order of the scientist who made him one, and becomes the next host. The player character changes from witness to material of the experiment.
