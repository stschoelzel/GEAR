# Observation style guide

[Deutsch](observation_style.de.md)

Rules for the section Observation in events. Part of schema 0.8; a change to these rules is a change of the schema version.

The aim is an observation that another person can check against the material and that a reader can interpret without being led. Based on low-inference descriptors from ethnography (LeCompte & Goetz 1982), the ADLAB audio description guidelines (Remael, Reviers & Vercauteren 2015) and the writing principles of ASD-STE100.

## Rules

1. **Describe what is perceivable.** States, actions, inputs and system responses that can be seen, heard or read. Nothing else.
2. **No intentions, effects or judgements.** "Forces", "manipulates", "wants to", "cruel", "disturbing" belong in Interpretation.
3. **Present tense, active voice, short sentences.** One action per sentence where possible.
4. **Precise verbs instead of adjectives.** "The prisoner screams and pulls at his chains", not "the prisoner suffers terribly".
5. **Name things consistently.** Use the name from the reference source. An unnamed figure is named by a stable visible feature ("the hooded figure") until the game names it.
6. **Keep channels apart.** Say whether something is shown, heard, written on screen, said in dialogue or done by input. Note where image and text agree or contradict each other.
7. **The player is "the player".** In German "die Spieler:in".
8. **Quote briefly and exactly.** In the language of the client, with locator or timecode. A translation may follow in brackets.
9. **Count and time where possible.** "Three uses", "after about 40 seconds".
10. **State uncertainty.** "Not checked whether", "unclear in the recording".

## Example

Not like this:

> The quest cruelly forces the player to torture a helpless prisoner until he breaks.

Like this:

> The player uses the Neural Needler on the Imprisoned Beryl Sorcerer. After each use a line of text from the prisoner appears. After several uses he names the place where Lady Evanor is held, and the objective is marked complete.

"Cruelly", "forces", "helpless" and "breaks" may all be right. They belong in Interpretation, where they can be argued.
